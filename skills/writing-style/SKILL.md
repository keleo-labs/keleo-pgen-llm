# Writing style

Read this before drafting or revising natural-language content for a human reader in any project skill. Apply it to reports, phase documents, practice descriptions and narratives, meeting briefings, slide copy and notes, issue text, documentation narrative and diagram labels. Do not apply it to code, JSON or YAML syntax, identifiers, quotations, official names or source text that must be preserved. For structured data, apply it to reader-facing string values only.

These are clarity rules adapted from [ASD-STE100 Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html), not a claim of formal ASD-STE100 conformance. Formal conformance requires the complete standard, its controlled dictionary and a dedicated review. Preserve technical accuracy and the author's meaning when a strict word or sentence limit would distort them.

## Draft and revise

1. Use one term for one concept. Prefer familiar words, define an unfamiliar term when first needed, and keep product and technical names consistent. Do not change terms merely for variety.
2. Keep noun groups short. Replace a stack of modifiers with a short phrase that shows the relationship between the nouns.
3. Use a clear verb and a complete sentence. Prefer active voice; use passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Do not remove necessary words just to shorten a sentence.
4. Put ideas in a logical order. Introduce a concept before its details. Give each paragraph one topic and keep it to six sentences or fewer.
5. Aim for at most 25 words in a descriptive sentence and 20 words in an instruction. Split multiple ideas or actions, but retain conditions and qualifications needed for accuracy. Treat these as revision targets for general prose, not mechanical rejection thresholds.
6. Write procedures in action order. Give one instruction per step, start with the action verb, and put a condition before the action when the reader must know it first. State a risk and its preventive action clearly in safety instructions.
7. Prefer simple punctuation. Split sentences joined by a semicolon when they contain separate ideas.

Before delivery, check the reader-facing text for inconsistent terms, dense noun groups, unclear actors, long sentences, misplaced conditions and paragraphs with multiple topics. Fix the specific passage without flattening the author's voice or changing a sourced claim. A report, deck or issue skill can add audience, citation and layout rules; those rules still apply.
