# Facilitation lenses

Recipe version: 2.0.1. Use supported lenses in the shared agenda and Analysis.
Apply a lens only where evidence supports it; do not stage an unnecessary workshop.

The [group evidence guide](group-evidence.md)
identifies saved publications and their limits. Read relevant passages before
citing; lecture material supplies context, not a citable publication. Record which
sources actually informed a brief and do not invent source consultation.

## In the room or with the owner

From Enberg, Lindkvist, and Tell: a team call coordinates. Specialist work continues with the owner.

During analysis distinguish **in the room** from **owner continues**.
In the room means the group must align, decide or integrate a transferable
learning. Owner continues means the work is moving and its update belongs in
the pre-read. Hearing routine status alone does not justify a live slot.

## A decision that is stuck

From Frisch: if the notes show a choice with no closure, the run-of-show separates the outcome from the path. Ask what must be true, then what options exist, what is negotiable, and what people prefer. Use a devil’s advocate so the critique is of the option, not the person. Recombine options before asking for a decision.

If nothing is stalled, do not add this. Trust exercises do not replace the process.

## How you run the room

From the super-facilitator work: agree who facilitates. Recognise evidenced contributions
when available. Have people note a point independently before anyone speaks,
invite different perspectives and offer your own position after others where
possible. Do not label someone quiet from sparse notes or cast another person
as facilitator unless agreed. Use Marquet's intent-based ownership within actual
competence and authority; a proposed owner must accept the commitment.

## What people got right

Name specific, shareable contributions from notes, rewards or feedback. Personal
criticism and manager reflection stay outside every section of the team report.

Never write “bad apple”, “underperformer”, or “out-group” about a person. The brief is saved and can be re-read later.

## Priorities for a new quarter

From Curşeu, Janssen, and Meeus, on a quarter opener only: propose a few shared priorities that are specific enough to miss, and that are not a restatement of business as usual. Where the notes show uneven ownership, ask how the team will support the least-owned priority. Do not rank people. On a weekly sync or a mid-quarter All Hands, do not run this goal-setting workshop. A weekly sync may flag one stated goal that is vague, trivial, or impossible, and otherwise leave goals alone.
