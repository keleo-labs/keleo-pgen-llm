## 1. The STAR Format

> **Overview:** Enforces a strict cause-and-effect relationship between context and outcomes.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Situation:** Describe the specific situation, context, or baseline state before action is taken.


* **Task:** Outline the specific task, problem, or goal that needs to be addressed.


* **Action:** Detail the methodology, activities, or steps taken to address the task.


* **Result:** Summarize the measurable results, outcomes, or state changes achieved from the actions.



---

## 2. The Hero's Journey

> **Overview:** Highly effective for macro-level lifecycle orchestrations such as platform adoptions and organizational transformations.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **The Ordinary World:** Describe the current, pre-platform state of the organization.


* **The Call to Adventure:** Outline the business opportunity, crisis, or driver that necessitates the platform transformation.


* **The Ordeal:** Detail the complex adoption activities, migrations, and challenges faced during implementation.


* **The Return:** Describe the successful adoption, the accrual of benefits, and the new optimized way of working.



---

## 3. The Three-Act Structure & StoryBrand

> **Overview:** Positions the consumer as the "Hero" and the Platform Engineering team as the "Guide" utilizing a defined approach.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Act I: The Hero and the Problem:** Identify the primary consumer (the Hero) and detail the specific friction or problem they face.


* **Act II: The Guide and the Plan:** Introduce the Platform Engineering team as the Guide who provides a clear, paved-path approach or plan.


* **Act III: Call to Action and Success:** Describe the call to action for the consumer to adopt the plan, and the successful outcome of doing so.



---

## 4. Micro-Narratives (ABT)

> **Overview:** Shorter frameworks (And/But/Therefore) designed for rapid, highly persuasive daily execution updates.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **And (Context):** State the current agreed-upon context, background, and baseline facts.


* **But (Conflict):** Introduce the specific problem, blocker, challenge, or contradiction encountered.


* **Therefore (Resolution):** State the specific action taken, decision made, or conclusion reached to resolve the conflict.



---

## 5. User Story

> **Overview:** A brief, informal explanation of a software feature written directly from the perspective of the end user.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **As a [role]:** Use this to define constraints. Instead of using generic terms like "the user," specify roles like "First-time Visitor" or "System Administrator" to give context on technical ability, permissions, and needs.


* **I want to [perform some action]:** Focus strictly on the "what," not the "how". Give the functional capability without naming specific UI elements like clicking a colored button.


* **So that [I can achieve some goal]:** Use this to justify the work and explain the underlying problem or value. A compelling goal helps justify building the feature and enables developers to suggest better technical solutions.



---

## 6. Epic

> **Overview:** Provides a "north star" for the team, ensuring everyone knows why a large project exists and what success looks like.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Summary:** Create a clear, punchy title and a one-sentence elevator pitch describing the high-level initiative to provide an instant understanding for stakeholder roadmaps.


* **Strategic Intent:** Explain how the Epic aligns with broader business goals or quarterly objectives (OKRs) to justify the budget and resource allocation.


* **User Personas:** List the primary and secondary users impacted by the large-scale change to keep the overall scope focused.


* **High-Level Requirements:** Establish the broad capabilities the finished system must have, using them as themes to brainstorm individual User Stories later.


* **Success Metrics:** Define quantitative data points to establish the project's true "Definition of Done" based on outcomes rather than just finished code.



---

## 7. Lifecycle

> **Overview:** A standard lifecycle orchestration framework used to map out progression phases.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Prerequisites:** Define the Phase 0 or preparation steps, including baseline checks or prerequisite states, that must be achieved before the main lifecycle begins.


* **Lifecycle Phase:** Detail the active state transitions, required work products, and activities for this specific phase of the lifecycle progression.



---

## 8. Essay Narrative

> **Overview:** A cohesive narrative format that guides the reader carefully through the evidence for and against a proposition before reaching a final conclusion.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Introduction:** Explain how the topic sits within the framework of the subject area as a whole and outline the structure briefly without giving conclusions away early.


* **Defining Key Concepts:** Relate in your own words how central concepts and terms are to be interpreted within the context of the essay without relying on dictionary definitions.


* **Presenting Evidence:** Evaluate secondary works and balance viewpoints against each other. Be selective with evidence, use signposting language, and prioritize paraphrasing and summarizing over heavy quoting.


* **Conclusion:** Draw the threads of your argument together into a direct response to the title, avoiding the introduction of new evidence or underdeveloped ideas.



---

## 9. Report Narrative

> **Overview:** A formal, informative structure broken down into sections and sub-sections to order and communicate relevant information in an easily navigable way.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Executive Summary:** Write this concise, stand-alone summary last. Keep it under a page to outline the purpose, methodology, findings, conclusions, and recommendations so readers can decide if they need to read the full report.


* **Introduction:** State what the report is about, define how it meets the brief's requirements, and explain the background, scope, and research methods.


* **Methods:** Provide thorough, step-by-step details explaining how the investigation was carried out and why specific methods were chosen so that a reader could replicate the research.


* **Results and Findings:** Present the findings of your research as simply as possible using text, graphs, tables, or diagrams, saving any interpretation for the discussion.


* **Discussion:** Show how the findings relate to the report's purpose and previous research. Interpret results, respond to the brief, and mention how unexpected problems were solved.


* **Recommendations:** Suggest authoritative, certain courses of action based on the evidence and conclusions to solve problems or develop a new strategy.

## 10. SDLC (Software Development Lifecycle)

> **Overview:** A pattern for systematic technical delivery from requirements gathering all the way through long-term maintenance.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Plan:** Define the objectives, scope, requirements, and success criteria for the initiative.


* **Design:** Establish the technical architecture, design patterns, and overall implementation approach.


* **Build:** Develop, configure, and assemble the necessary solution components.


* **Test:** Verify functionality, performance, security, and integration through rigorous validation and quality assurance.


* **Deploy:** Roll out the solution to the production environment using an appropriate deployment strategy.


* **Maintain:** Monitor, optimize, patch, and evolve the solution while operational in production.



---

## 11. PDCA (Deming Cycle)

> **Overview:** A continuous loop framework designed for continuous improvement and iterative refinement.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Plan:** Identify an improvement opportunity, analyze the current state, and plan the intervention or process.


* **Do:** Execute the planned change or experiment on a small scale within a controlled environment.


* **Check:** Measure and evaluate outcomes against your original objectives to identify gaps and key learnings.


* **Act:** Scale successful changes, standardize processes, or adjust the plan based on findings before repeating the cycle.



---

## 12. Build-Measure-Learn

> **Overview:** A Lean Startup cycle designed to validate critical assumptions through rapid experimentation and feedback.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Build:** Create the smallest possible increment or minimum viable solution required to test a key hypothesis or assumption.


* **Measure:** Collect quantitative and qualitative evidence by instrumenting, observing, and gathering user feedback.


* **Learn:** Analyze and synthesize findings to validate or invalidate the hypothesis, deciding whether to pivot or persevere.



---

## 13. Design-Build-Run-Optimize

> **Overview:** An architectural lifecycle tailored for infrastructure and platform engineering, spanning from initial conception to continuous improvement.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Design:** Define the architecture, topology, technology choices, and capacity planning.


* **Build:** Deploy the infrastructure, configure systems, and establish required integrations and connectivity.


* **Run:** Manage steady-state operations, monitor health, respond to incidents, and maintain overall performance and availability.


* **Optimize:** Tune performance, reduce costs, and enhance capabilities based on direct operational insights.



---

## 14. Crawl-Walk-Run

> **Overview:** A maturity progression model used for building and adopting capabilities incrementally.
> 
> 

### How to Write:

* **Crawl:** Establish foundational capabilities with limited scope, utilizing manual processes and focusing heavily on initial learning.


* **Walk:** Refine and scale capabilities by expanding scope, introducing automation, and standardizing processes to build operational confidence.


* **Run:** Achieve mature, optimized operations featuring full automation, enterprise-wide scale, and continuous evolution.
